# NLSY79: gradient of ever having a child in ability and earnings

Generated by `data/spatial/nlsy_fertility_gradient.py` (B = 200 HHID-cluster bootstrap draws, seed 20261019). No microdata. SEs in parentheses.

## Definitions

- Outcome y = 1 if ever had a biological child (`nlsy_fertility.py`): completed-fertility sample = last valid NUMKID observation at age >= 45, POP_SAMPLES, last-round SAMPWEIGHT/100; both genders pooled and by gender. Mean p = 0.802 (SE 0.005).
- x = `afqt_c` from `nlsy_ability.block_a`: equal-weight mean of the four standardised ASVAB IRT z-scores, re-standardised (1979 weights) in the Block A population sample (mean 0, SD 1 there). Reliability R_comp_pop = 0.9158. Latent slope = observed slope / sqrt(R) (classical measurement error; same correction as block C). Within the fertility sample SD(x) is 1.004 (pooled).
- Logit: weighted logit of y on x; 'latent (approx)' = b_obs / sqrt(R), a first-order approximation (exact only for a linear probability model). 'Latent (deconvolved)' solves E[logistic(a + b x)] = p and E[logistic(a + b x) x] = latent LPM slope for x ~ N(0,1), i.e. it matches the population-average derivative of the logistic to the measurement-error-corrected LPM slope (no small-b approximation).
- Own permanent earnings: mean over person-years at interview ages 30-44 (>= 3 valid years) of wage-and-salary income in the previous calendar year (`nlsy_ability._income79`: revised-topcode Q13-5), deflated to 2010 dollars with the pipeline's CPI-U (year Y-1); zeros (non-work years) are kept in the mean; log of the mean, dropped if the mean is < $1,000. Wage-rate variant: mean over years with income >= $1,000 and >= 500 annual hours of log(income / hours) (>= 2 years). Family income: same window, TNFI_TRUNC (total net family income, topcoded), same deflator, log of the mean over >= 3 years. Ranks are weighted mid-rank percentiles within the estimation sample of each row (pooled rank for the pooled row; 'within-gender' rank is computed separately for women and men in the pooled sample). Rank slopes are per 10 percentile points.
- Survey interviews are annual to 1994 and biennial after, so a 30-44 window holds up to 15 annual or about 10 mixed person-years; median # valid earnings years in the sample = 9.
- Income in the 30-44 window is largely observed AFTER the first birth for most parents, and family income is jointly determined with marriage and children. Women's own earnings also respond to children (reverse causality). Own AFQT (ASVAB administered in 1980, ages 15-23) is predetermined and is the cleaner gradient.

## 3. Mapping to the model

Model: P(child | x) = logistic(a + b x), x ~ N(0,1) latent log ability, mean P fixed at p = 0.802.

- Pooled latent LPM slope = -2.38 (0.61) pp per latent SD; sample mean p = 80.18%. Small-slope map b = slope / (p(1-p)) = **-0.150** (0.038).
- Direct weighted logit, latent (approx = b_obs / sqrt(R)): **-0.151 (0.039)**.
- Deconvolved logit slope (matches mean p and latent LPM slope exactly for x ~ N(0,1)): **-0.150 (0.038)**; intercept that keeps the population mean at p = 0.802: a = 1.406.
- By group (deconvolved b): pooled -0.150 (0.038); women -0.319 (0.055); men -0.031 (0.050).

Cross-check from the earnings gradient: (dP/d log earnings) x 0.213 (log earnings per latent SD of AFQT, block C target) = the ability slope that would arise if the entire earnings gradient were driven by ability (pp per latent SD). Compare with the direct latent LPM slope above.

## Caveats

- Men under-report fathered children (non-resident fathers, earlier relationships), which plausibly biases men's rates down, and more so at low AFQT / low earnings, so men's ability slope is likely biased toward positive values (and the earnings slope upward).
- afqt_c is held fixed in the bootstrap; SEs do not include uncertainty in R_comp_pop (SE 0.002) or in the factor fit.
- Earnings and family income are measured after most births and respond to them (especially women's earnings); the earnings gradients are descriptive, not causal.
- The latent logit corrections assume classical measurement error and a normal latent ability distribution.
