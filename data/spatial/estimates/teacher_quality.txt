E. RESIDUAL -- what the model must then attribute to teacher quality
==============================================================================
   Q_l = (2 H_T,l / M_l)^sigma  and  H_T,l = N_T,l * E_l[h^beta], so
       dlog E[h^beta] = dlog Q / sigma - dlog(teachers per pupil).
   dlog Q comes from the grade-equivalent learning rate, which is a
   genuine ratio; the CS learning rate is a deviation around the
   national average and has no level to take a ratio of.

   scheme       dlogQ  dlog(N_T/M)   sigma=0.3   sigma=0.5   sigma=0.7
   locale     +0.0322      -0.0133     +0.1207     +0.0777     +0.0593
   revenue    +0.0392      +0.0356     +0.0951     +0.0428     +0.0204
   ses        +0.0804      -0.0035     +0.2715     +0.1643     +0.1184
   (columns: the implied within-CZ log gap in beta-weighted teacher quality)

   Is that gap big or small?  Convert the growth gap into the units the
   teacher value-added literature reports -- SDs of student achievement
   gained from one teacher in one year -- and compare:

   scheme    growth gap/grade   as share of VA sd   vs FRL access gap
   locale             +0.0031               0.03x               0.13x
   revenue            +0.0071               0.06x               0.30x
   ses                +0.0145               0.13x               0.61x

   VA sd = 0.116 (CFR I, elementary math, shrunk);
   FRL access gap = 0.024 SD math (Isenberg et al. 2013, 29 districts).
   Read: even attributing the ENTIRE within-CZ growth gap to teachers,
   the district-mean teacher-quality gap the model needs is a few percent
   of a teacher-VA standard deviation on the city/suburb split.

F. AGGREGATION -- why no district series can identify teacher VA
==============================================================================
   teachers per district, unweighted: p10=19, p25=39, p50=87, p75=209, p90=476
   enrolment-weighted median = 718, mean = 2,431

   observed within-CZ sd of the district learning rate = 0.0278 SD/grade (noise removed)
   85% of teacher-VA variance is within school, not between (CFR I),
   and a district mean averages over hundreds of teachers:

    sd of teacher VA  teachers  sd of district mean  observed / that
               0.080        50               0.0113             2.5x
               0.080       200               0.0057             4.9x
               0.080      1000               0.0025            11.0x
               0.116        50               0.0164             1.7x
               0.116       200               0.0082             3.4x
               0.116      1000               0.0037             7.6x
               0.163        50               0.0231             1.2x
               0.163       200               0.0115             2.4x
               0.163      1000               0.0052             5.4x

   Read: district growth varies several times more than random
   allocation of teachers would produce, so there IS systematic sorting
   in it -- but what survives aggregation is teacher sorting bundled
   with curriculum, leadership, peers and everything else the district
   does.  No district-level series separates the bundle.

   Two regressions the main report runs on the locale partition only.
   The sample, not the data, is what makes the kappa gradient look dead:

   0.974, so putting it on the right-hand side attenuates a
   coefficient by only 2.7%.  Measurement error is not the
   story; sigma is genuinely not identified in this cross-section, and
   the kappa gradient is genuinely positive once all districts are used.
