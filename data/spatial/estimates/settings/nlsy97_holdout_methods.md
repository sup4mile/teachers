## 3. Definitions

**Ability (own AFQT).** The NLSY79 `afqt_c` is the equal-weight mean of the four age-normed ASVAB IRT z-scores (AR, WK, PC, MK), each standardized and the mean re-standardized, 1979 weights. The NLSY97 counterpart uses the four CAT-ASVAB ability estimates (`ASVAB_{AR,WK,PC,MK}_ABILITY_EST_POS/NEG`, 1997-98), age-normed exactly as `nlsy_ability.build_n97` does for block V (weighted rank-normal within birth-year x quarter cells, round-1 `SAMPLING_WEIGHT_CC`), then the equal-weight mean of the four standardized z-scores, re-standardized with the draw weights (`nlsy_ability.pop_afqt`). Sample: respondents with all four scores and a positive weight (N = 7093, the figure behind the published R_comp_97). **Latent units.** A difference of group means is divided by sqrt(R_comp_97), the one-factor ULS reliability of the composite, re-estimated in every draw (the NLSY79 gaps use sqrt(R_comp_pop)). Terciles: weighted quantiles (`nlsy_extensions.tercile_of`).

**Items 1-3 (residential transitions).** The T3 NLSY97 machinery of `nlsy_extensions.py` (`build_trans97`, `stats_from`) through `nlsy_composition.trans_stats`. Origin: `CV_MSA` at the 1997 interview for respondents aged 12-16 (central city = city, in MSA but not central city = suburb; not in MSA and 'not known' are outside the 2x2). Adult: every round at ages 25-34 (`CV_AGE_INT_DATE`; rounds 2004-2019) with a classified `CV_MSA`, which from 2004 codes residence in a CBSA (metropolitan plus micropolitan; see the departures table); each person carries the mean of his or her round weights. The 2x2 sample is persons classified city or suburb at both dates (6221 persons, 37502 person-years; the same as the published NLSY97 table). Terciles of own ability are cut over respondents aged 12-16 at the 1997 interview with a valid score (the analogue of `afqt_origin`), weights `w0` x multiplicity; statistics by tercile: city to suburb, suburb to city, move rate, origin city share (all within the 2x2 sample), and top minus bottom. Variants (`transv|…`): origin at age 12 (parent and youth reports), same-region movers only, adult windows 25-27, 28-32, rounds 2004-2011, 2013-2019, the *latest* classified round at age 25 or older (any age up to 43; the 'latest available' reading of the request), adult rounds with `CV_URBAN-RURAL` = urban only (`adult_urban`, a partial sensitivity to the change of universe: urban areas include micropolitan cores, so it does not isolate metropolitan residence), the tercile assignment by NLS's own percentile `ASVAB_MATH_VERBAL_SCORE_PCT`, and `origin_all|…` (origin city share among everyone with a known city/suburb origin, no adult requirement).

**Extra: adult suburb share.** Share of person-rounds in the suburb among person-rounds at 25-34 in a CBSA with a known central-city status, by tercile of own ability cut over the whole sample with a valid score (`afqt_full`), `nlsy_composition.adult_stats`; sample = every respondent observed at 25-34 (`adult`), the 2x2 transition sample (`adult_2x2`) or urban rounds only (`adult_urban`).

**Item 4 (teachers).** Occupation of the *main job* in each round at 25-34: `CV_MAINJOB_FLG` is the roster position of the current or most recent employer at the interview, `YEMP_OCCODE-2002.NN` the 2002 Census 4-digit code of roster job NN. K-12 teachers are codes 2300 (pre-K and kindergarten), 2310 (elementary and middle), 2320 (secondary), 2330 (special education) and 2340 (other teachers and instructors), the project's ACS definition (`nlsy_teachers.TEACHER_CODES['c02']`; the labels are those of the 2002 Census listing in NLSY79 Codebook Supplement Attachment 3, `att300.pdf`, https://www.nlsinfo.org/sites/default/files/attachments/121217/att300.pdf; postsecondary teachers (2200) and teacher assistants (2540) are not counted). A round counts if it is not an armed-forces round (below) and the code is a civilian occupation (10-9750); the 23 rounds with a 'military specific' code (9800-9840) are at non-military employers, are not counted as an occupation and stay in the denominators. Samples as in `nlsy_teachers.py`: `emp` = rounds in which the main job is held at the interview date (`YEMP_CURFLAG` of the main job = 1; the analogue of `ESR_COL` = 1), `any` = every round with a valid occupation (current or most recent job), `pop` = every respondent observed at 25-34, a person with no occupation counting as a non-teacher. A person is a K-12 teacher if more than half of his or her counted rounds are K-12 ('maj'; 'half' counts ties as teachers, 'ever' = at least one round). Comparison groups A, B, C as in the NLSY79 report (B and C use college, below). Weights: round-1 weight (`SAMPLING_WEIGHT_CC` 1997), one observation per person. The specification grid is that of `nlsy_teachers.make_specs` (all/core definitions x three rules, by type, highest grade >= 9, cross-sectional sample only (`CV_SAMPLE_TYPE` = 1), unweighted, mean round weights, for `emp` and `any`; three rules for `pop`).

**Armed forces (the `ESR_COL` = 4 analogue).** `CV_MAINJOB_FLG` never points to a military employer (0 of 1375 person-rounds with a military employer on the roster), so active-duty rounds have no main job and leave `emp` and `any` by construction; the earlier occupation-code rule (9800-9840) did not identify them (it dropped rounds at non-military employers) and the rounds stayed in the `pop` sample, the teaching-share denominators and the schooling samples, which the NLSY79 drops through `ESR_COL` = 4. A round is now an armed-forces round if a military roster employer (`YEMP_MILFLAG` = 1) is held at the interview (`YEMP_CURFLAG` = 1) and no civilian employer is. 948 person-rounds (250 persons) are dropped from every sample; 87 persons are observed only in such rounds. A reserve or guard member who also holds a civilian job (211 rounds) is a civilian worker, as in the CPS and in NLS's own `CV_DOI_EMPLOYED` (2 = 'military service (but no job)', 2015+ rounds). The rule and `CV_DOI_EMPLOYED` agree in 8690 of 8694 person-rounds with a valid `CV_DOI_EMPLOYED` (107 flagged by both).

**Item 5 (schooling).** Two college definitions: `c16` = 16 or more years of schooling (`CV_HGC_EVER_EDT`, maximum over the person's rounds at 25-34, as `nlsy_teachers.py` uses the highest `HGCREV`: the NLSY79 definition) and `ba` = bachelor's degree or higher (`CV_HIGHEST_DEGREE_EVER_EDT`, maximum over the rounds at 25-34, codes 4-7; the NLSY79 extract has no degree variable). Sample: persons observed at 25-34 (an armed-forces-only person excluded, as the NLSY79 `observed`) with the schooling variable known. **Headline universe = that of the NLSY79 objects of `education_occupation.py`** (`evaluate_ba`, sample `all_hgc9`, the ACS Table 2 universe): highest grade >= 9, terciles of ability cut over that sample itself with the draw weights (`edu9_cutm|c16|share|…`, pooled; men and women are shares within the pooled terciles), and the gap in mean latent ability between 16+ years and the rest of the same sample (`edu9|c16|{pooled,men,women}|gap`). Alternatives: the same with `ba` (the ACS-style definition); the earlier headline universe (`edu|…`: no grade rule, terciles cut over the whole sample with a valid score, the `afqt_full` convention of `nlsy_composition.py`; 220 persons with a valid score and a highest grade below 9 (`c16`; for `ba`, below 9 or unknown: 271) are in `edu` and out of `edu9`, which is where the two universes differ); `edu_cutm` (no grade rule, terciles over the analysis sample). Gaps are in latent SD units.

**Teaching shares (check).** Employed K-12 person-rounds over all person-rounds at 25-34 with highest grade >= 9 (home production included, armed-forces rounds excluded), `nlsy_teachers.share_stats`; compare with the ACS 2009-13 shares of the calibration (NLSY97 respondents are 25-34 in 2004-2019).

## 4. Inference

Two bootstraps, B = 500 each, on the same statistics; both re-standardize the composite, re-estimate R_comp_97 and recompute all tercile cuts in every draw, hold the age-norming of the raw CAT-ASVAB estimates fixed (as `nlsy_ability.py` block V), use the same multipliers for every statistic (common random numbers, so correlations across statistics are valid), and store the SD of the draws as the SE and the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles as a 95% interval.

1. **Household clusters** (`se`, `ci95_lo`, `ci95_hi`, `n_draws`; seed 20260929): round-1 households resampled with replacement. NLSY97 households are the connected components of the *reciprocated* round-1 roster links `YOUTH_SIBID01-04.01` (6817 households for 8984 respondents; sizes {1: 4955, 2: 1595, 3: 235, 4: 26, 5: 6}; 5037 directed links between two respondents, 5026 reciprocated and used, 11 not reciprocated and ignored, none pointing outside the sample; 1 respondent listed as his or her own household member, ignored). NLS documents 6,819 unique households, 1,862 of them with more than one respondent (https://www.nlsinfo.org/content/cohorts/nlsy97/intro-to-the-sample/sample-design-screening-process); the 1862 multi-respondent components here match that figure, so the difference of 2 households is in single-respondent households (two respondents who sit in a multi-respondent component here would be alone in NLS's count; the public file does not resolve which). Two of the 11 unreciprocated links join respondents in different design PSUs, so using them (the earlier version did) merged two households that are not one and broke the nesting of households in PSUs; no household spans a PSU now (0). This is the analogue of the NLSY79 `HHID` cluster bootstrap and is kept for comparability with the NLSY79 SEs.

2. **Design-based PSU bootstrap** (`se_psu`, `ci95_lo_psu`, `ci95_hi_psu`, `n_draws_psu`; seed 20260929 + 97). The NLSY97 is an area sample, and the public file carries `VSTRAT` and `VPSU` (NLS: 'variable for use with variance PSU to correct for clustering in the sample design'; 117 strata, two PSUs each, 234 PSUs; respondents per PSU 7 to 111, median 33.5). City/suburb residence is strongly clustered by PSU, so household-cluster SEs understate the sampling error of the residential statistics (section 7 reports the ratio of the two SEs for the published parental-income table, on which no ability statistic is involved). Each draw is a stratified Rao-Wu (1988) rescaling bootstrap: in every stratum one of the two PSUs is drawn (n_h - 1 = 1 draw) and its respondents' weights are multiplied by n_h/(n_h - 1) = 2, the other PSU's by 0. Households nest in PSUs, so they need no separate resampling; for a weighted total this reproduces the standard two-PSU-per-stratum variance estimator. **Use `se_psu` for any test of the NLSY97 against the model**; `se` is for comparison with the NLSY79 SEs, which the NLSY79 design (household clusters) allows.

## 7. Checks run before sealing (no sealed value involved)

`python3 nlsy97_holdout.py --check` prints the sample sizes and PASS/FAIL for: the published `R_comp_97` and the published NLSY97 parental-income table (`nlsy_extensions.json`, persons as clusters, seed 20260929) rebuilt from this extract; the adult rows of `build_trans97_adult` equal those of `ne.build_trans97`; the design variables (117 strata x 2 PSUs, households nested in PSUs, Rao-Wu multipliers with mean one); the generic gap, teaching-share and schooling functions reproduce `nlsy_teachers.evaluate` / `share_stats` and the published `education_occupation.json` BA-by-tercile and gap objects on the NLSY79 data (at the sample and, for the teacher functions, at one bootstrap draw) and the published `nlsy_teachers.json`; `terciles()` reproduces `nlsy_composition.groupings`; the transition statistics reproduce the published `nlsy_composition.json`; and structural properties of the NLSY97 statistics (finite, shares in [0, 1], ordered cuts, tercile masses). `--selftest` runs synthetic unit tests (including the Rao-Wu variance identity, the household components and the armed-forces rule), and `--verify-seal` prints the structure (not the values) of the sealed file. `--seal` runs the same checks first and refuses to seal if one fails; it prints one line, `sealed: <path> (<k> moments)`, and refuses to overwrite an existing sealed file without `--force`. **Scope of the cross-check.** The checks above validate the generic code on NLSY79 data. `nlsy97_holdout_xcheck.py` recomputes 66 of the sealed statistics (transitions by tercile, the teacher gaps A, B and C of the main specification for pooled, men and women, the BA+ / 16+ share by tercile and the BA+ vs non-BA gap in both universes, R_comp_97) in separately written pandas code at the sample (against the sealed point estimates), at the first draw of each bootstrap (against `evaluate97`) and over both whole bootstraps (against the sealed SEs and 95% limits), printing only PASS/FAIL and the maximum absolute difference. In this version it also derives the household clusters (breadth-first search on the reciprocated links), the PSU index and the armed-forces rounds independently from the raw columns. It does not check what the raw variables *mean* (the codebook readings: CBSA vs MSA, `CV_MAINJOB_FLG`, `YEMP_CURFLAG`, `CV_HGC_EVER_EDT`), which the earlier version, reusing `Frame97`'s household index and reading the variables the same way, could not either; those readings are documented in this file and were reviewed against the codebook.

## 9. Caveats

- The NLSY97 occupation is the main job of the round, which is not the NLSY79 CPS item: a person with two jobs is classified by the roster position NLS flags as main, and an 'any' round can be a job that ended before the interview.
- Respondents' later rounds (after 34) are not used for teachers or schooling; the youngest respondents contribute fewer rounds at 25-34 than the oldest.
- Residential transitions: the adult rounds are classified by CBSA (metropolitan plus micropolitan) and the origin by metropolitan MSA, so the 2x2 universe differs between the two dates and the NLSY79 adult universe is metropolitan only (departures table). The move rates and the adult suburb share can be biased in either direction by this, and so can their by-tercile gradients; only the mechanical reclassification at the MSA-standard changes has a known sign (it raises move-rate levels). Cross-MSA moves cannot be separated. The origin-to-adult comparison is more favorable to the NLSY79 rate in cleanliness and to the NLSY97 rate in sample size.
- `se` (household clusters) understates the sampling error of clustered outcomes, above all residence; the design-based `se_psu` is the SE to use against the model. `se_psu` is conditional on NLS's variance PSUs, which are constructed for variance estimation (two per stratum) and are not the 147 sampled PSUs; both SEs treat the age-norming of the raw CAT-ASVAB estimates as known.
- Respondents without all four CAT-ASVAB scores (about a fifth) are outside every statistic that uses ability, and no correction is made for them or for attrition (round-1 weights, as `SAMPWEIGHT` 1979 in the NLSY79 analyses).
- The NLSY79 public file has no PSU or stratum variable (its only design-related variables are `HHID` and `SAMPLE_ID`), so the NLSY79 SEs of the calibration targets can only be household-clustered; for residential statistics they may be understated in the same way as the household-cluster SEs here.
- With half of the PSUs given weight 0 in every design draw, thin cells (men in pre-K or special-education teaching) are empty in more draws than in the household bootstrap: see `n_draws_psu`.
- This file reports no value; the checks above use only published or unsealed NLSY79 and NLSY97 results.
