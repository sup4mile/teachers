**Goods investment.** Spending over labor income is $S=\eta(1-t)+t$, where $t$ is the tax funding teacher payroll, so $\eta=(S-t)/(1-t)$. For 2018, $S=0.060/0.591=0.102$ (education spending over the PWT labor share), and $t\approx0.023$ (3.2 million FTE teachers at \$60,483, benefits at 0.43 of salary, against 2018 GDP of \$20.66 trillion); unrounded, $\eta=0.0807$ (`eta_2018`). The old 0.103 is the gross $S$, which counts teacher pay twice. Counting all instructional staff gives $t=0.030$, $\eta=0.073$. At the first internal fit, teacher payroll is 2.5% of market income, against $t=0.023$ (4.2% at the T1 reference solution); the solver's own $t$ (1.6%) also taxes home-production output, which is 34% of model income.

**Time investment.** With zero goods moving costs, $s_O=\mu\phi/(\mu\phi+1-\eta)$ and $s_T=\mu\phi\gamma/(\mu\phi\gamma+1-\gamma\eta)$, which depend only on $\mu\phi$. Location choice and WTP depend only on ratios to $\mu$, and $\bar\kappa$ absorbs its remaining role, so $\mu=1$ and $\phi$ matches non-teachers' schooling ($s=$ years/25). With $r_m>0$, evaluate at the reference $\Xi$.

**Schooling (T2).** From `data/Mincer_YrsSchool.xlsx` (sheet `years_of_school`, 2013 column: ACS 2009–13, ages 25–34, years capped at 9–17), averaged over occupation-by-gender cells with the Table 2 counts: non-teachers, home production included, 13.64 years (men 13.44, women 13.84) and K–12 teachers 16.04 (both genders), so $s_O=0.5457$ and $s_T/s_O=1.176$. The $\Xi=0$ formula gives $\phi=1.105$; the goods moving cost raises time shares through $\Xi$, and `calibrate_phi` sets $\phi=1.0584$ so that non-teachers' mean $s_O$ at the reference solution is 0.5457 (C̄, h̄ iterated jointly). The ACS microdata pull (T4) cross-checks the workbook.

**Known miss.** With a common $\phi$ and $\gamma<1$, teachers get less schooling than non-teachers: $s_T/s_O=0.91$ at $\vartheta_0$ 0.75 at the first fit ($\gamma=0.61$; $s_T=0.41$, $s_O=0.55$) and 0.76 at the exactly identified fit, against 1.18 in the data (this note's earlier 1.23 is not reproduced by the workbook). Report this as untargeted. The implied teacher leisure advantage is absorbed by $\bar\kappa$. $\phi$ is set at $\vartheta_0$, so at the fit $s_O$ is 0.550 against 0.546.

**Exposure.** Per-year evidence pins $\sigma/K$ and $\beta/K$, while $\beta/\sigma$ is free of $K$, so $K$ sets how much schools matter. $K=5$ (SEDA's tested grades) is a sensitivity case.

**Location tastes.** Without housing, effective dispersion is roughly $\sigma_\nu/\mu$ plus housing share over supply elasticity ($\approx0.45$), so 0.25 likely overstates population responses; sensitivity extends to 0.50.

**Units.** Freeze benchmark $\bar C$ and $\bar h$, which scale the moving cost and warm glow. Keep consumption, moving costs and WTP in the same units. They are frozen at $\vartheta_0$ in `spatial_reference.toml` ($\bar C=0.044114$, $\bar h=0.095780$, refrozen 2026-09-29 after the solver correction of §4 and $\phi=1.0584$; 0.041950 and 0.090917 after T2b; 0.012554 and 0.021000 before) and depend on Table 2 and $\phi$, so refreeze whenever either changes.

Forty-one parameters, forty-one moments. No education wedges and no wage wedges for men or teaching. The levels of $A_i$ and of the female outside option relative to teaching are internal (Table 3).

**Ability block.** Estimate $(\sigma_\epsilon,\rho_z,s_z)$ in a first stage outside equilibrium, holding $Q_l$ and $t_l$ fixed and ignoring selection into teaching for both genders in shares and wage distributions. Within a cell, $Q_l$ and $t_l$ are constants. In this first-pass approximation, non-teachers' log wages are $b[\alpha\log z+\log(\Theta_{i,g}\epsilon_i^\alpha)]$ plus a constant, and $z$ is independent of the chosen non-teaching occupation, gender and $\epsilon$. The common underlying ability distribution also applies to teaching draws in the full model; selected teachers need not have the same ability distribution as non-teachers. Each moment then pins one parameter under the first-pass approximation. The model's latent mother–child score correlation is $\rho_z$, so $\rho_z=0.577$. The slope per latent SD is $b\,\alpha s_z$, so $s_z=0.21266(1-\eta)/\alpha=0.1956$ at the model's $\eta=0.080$ (0.1955 at the unrounded 0.0807 in `implied_s_z`). $\sigma_\epsilon$ solves the pooled 90/10 given $s_z$, with the shares block inverted at each value; the 90/10 depends on both $z$ and $\epsilon$, while scores measure $z$ but not $\epsilon_i$, which separates them. Both data targets are latent (the factor-model correlation and the slope over $\sqrt{R}$), so no simulated panels or score noise are needed; $\mathrm{var}(u)$, from the composite reliabilities (0.92 for mothers, 0.89 for children), matters only for composite-level checks. The model's latent sibling correlation is $\rho_z^2\approx0.33$, against 0.606 in the data (check only). Use individual earnings (no spouses in the model) and read $\rho_z$ as reduced form, including assortative mating. What the first stage leaves out goes to the section 4 consistency check: sorting on $Q_l$ in scores (signal $\log z+c\log Q_l$) raises the model correlation above $\rho_z$; cross-location variation in $Q_l$ and $t_l$ adds wage dispersion, lowering $\sigma_\epsilon$; and teaching selection changes non-teachers' shares and wage distributions and can alter the wage–score slope, while teachers themselves (about 4% of the slope sample) load on $z$ with a different elasticity.

**Ability grid.** Rouwenhorst matches the stationary variance and autocorrelation of $\log z$ exactly at any Nz, so $\rho_z$ and $s_z$ do not depend on it; the 90/10, a quantile, does. Compute the first stage on the full model's Rouwenhorst grid, not a continuous normal, so it hands the GE the distribution the GE uses. Compare Nz = 5, 9 and 15 with the continuous-$z$ limit and set the full model's Nz (`SOLVER` in `spatial_calibrate.jl`, now 5) where the 90/10 and $\hat\sigma_\epsilon$ stop moving; confirm it in the GE (section 4), since solve time grows with Nz. In the first stage, $\hat\sigma_\epsilon$ is 0.7852, 0.7861, 0.7866 and 0.7867 at Nz = 3, 5, 9 and 15, against 0.7870 in the continuous limit. At the limit's $\hat\sigma_\epsilon$, Nz = 5 overstates the pooled 90/10 by 0.005 (0.1%), so Nz stays at 5.

**Scale and $\chi$ (decided).** $s_z$ is free and targets the NLSY slope, which matches the model object: latent scores and log hourly wages at 25–34, with the ACS weeks/hours/real-income cutoffs (remaining sample differences below). CFR's 12% is a descriptive association, and each of its definitions (single-year observed scores in grades 3–8, earnings at 28 with zeros as a share of the mean, controls) pushes it below $b\,s_z$. $\chi$ only converts school-driven score changes (SEDA, WTP): a score SD from $Q_l$ is worth $b\,s_z/c$, so $c=b\,s_z/\chi\approx1.88$, also used in the score counterparts. T6a supports this reading: CFR's 12% conditions on lagged scores (and teacher fixed effects); in the CNLSY, earnings at 28 relative to the mean rise 0.23 per observed score SD without lagged scores and 0.118 with lagged-score cubics and parental controls, reproducing CFR, while ACS-style log wages give 0.18–0.20 per latent SD without lags, close to the NLSY79's 0.213 ([NLSY extensions](../../../data/spatial/estimates/nlsy_extensions.md)). The gap between $b\,s_z$ and $\chi$ is mostly conditional versus unconditional, so $\chi$ stays a conditional (school-driven) effect. The alternative $b\,s_z=\chi$ ($s_z=0.104$, about 13 SEs below the slope) is in Table 5. It cuts the log-wage variance contributed by $z$ by about 72% before refitting $\sigma_\epsilon$; sorting and teacher selection scale with $s_z$.

For survey year $Y$, the nominal floor is $1000\times\mathrm{CPIU}_{Y-1}/\mathrm{CPIU}_{2010}$, using [BLS annual CPI-U](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/supplemental-files/historical-cpi-u-202312.pdf); $\mathrm{CPIU}_{2010}=218.056$. This uses the income reference year, not the interview year. Nominal log wages can remain in the regression because year dummies absorb the deflator. The previous 50-week/35-hour, positive-income, 1/99-trimmed specification gave 0.196 on 34,118 NLSY79 person-years; the corrected baseline gives 0.213 on 37,870 person-years (7,219 people; 95% CI [0.200, 0.229]). Retaining 1/99 trimming with the new cutoffs gives 0.196 (SE 0.007), reported only as a sensitivity. NLSY97 rises from 0.175 to 0.199 (SE 0.009; 19,782 person-years).

NLSY uses age at interview and prior-calendar-year income/hours/weeks, with weekly hours constructed as annual hours divided by weeks worked. Existing status and education filters also differ: NLSY79 excludes armed forces (ESR = 4) but does not drop current unemployment or missing ESR; NLSY97 has no military-status filter in this extract and retains missing highest grade. These differences remain explicit limitations of the comparison.

*Cell 90/10s (check only) are missed.* A common $\sigma_\epsilon$ compresses the spread across cells: model cell 90/10s lie in [3.21, 4.15], against [2.80, 6.59] in the data, and their weighted correlation with the data across the 38 cells is −0.19. **Why no equilibrium.** Among non-teaching alternatives, agents choose the occupation maximizing $(1-\tau^\omega_{i,g})A_i\epsilon_i^\alpha$. With teaching selection ignored, $z$, $Q_l$, taxes, teacher pay, amenities, altruism and moving costs drop out of these shares. The first pass uses observed shares conditional on not teaching as targets for this 20-occupation Roy problem and ignores teaching selection consistently in both genders' wage distributions. Teaching shares of 1.9% for men and 6.0% for women motivate this approximation but do not bound its error, especially within individual occupations or wage tails. No auxiliary teaching-selection rule is required for T2b. After the first internal fit, the section 4 consistency check measures the effects of teaching selection and spatial variation; any correction is deferred until that check shows it matters.

3. Table 2 and the teaching margin use ACS 2009–13, against late-2010s spatial targets. Wage dispersion was rising (90/10 of 3.36 in 2000), and men's home-production share (22.9%, against 18.6% in 2000) reflects the recession, lowering market $A_i$ and the levels set against them, including $\bar\kappa$. ## 2. Internal calibration

Write $\kappa_1=\bar\kappa$, $\kappa_2=\bar\kappa e^{\delta_\kappa}$, $B=(0,\Delta B)$, $m_{12}=m_{21}=r_m\bar C$ and $1-\tau^\omega_{i,f}=\omega_f r_i$. The pay level enters as $\bar\kappa=\tilde\kappa\,\bar h^{1-\gamma}$, so a teacher with the reference human capital $\bar h$ earns $\tilde\kappa\bar h$ in the city at any $\gamma$; without this normalization the admissible $\bar\kappa$ shifts with $\gamma$ (since $h<1$), a diagonal valley for the search. Fit all parameters jointly; each row names the main source of identification. District moments are from [spatial_moments.md](../../../data/spatial/estimates/spatial_moments.md).

## 3. Mapping evidence to model objects

**Teacher quality (CFR).** With teacher headcount $n_l$ and student mass $S_l=M_l/2$,

$$
Q_l=\left[\frac{n_l}{S_l}\,\mathbb E(h_T^{\beta/\sigma}\mid T,l)\right]^\sigma .
$$

The efficient class-size allocation leaves no equilibrium variation in teacher value added, so match CFR with an auxiliary intervention: raise one teacher's log human capital by one SD for one year, holding class size and location fixed. For a non-teaching child,

$$
\Delta\log y'\simeq\frac{\beta\,s_{\log h_T}}{K(1-\eta)}\;\longrightarrow\;\log(1.013).
$$

This assumes one SD of teacher human capital equals one SD of causal effectiveness.

**Class size.** FOO (2013, Table V, col. 1) find log wages fall 0.0063 (SE 0.0033) per extra pupil over grades 4–6. With three years of exposure and mean class size 24.357,

$$
\sigma\simeq(1-\eta)\frac{K}{3}(24.357)(0.0063)=0.565
\quad\text{at }\eta=0.080,\ K=12,
$$

with a 95% interval of $[-0.015,\,1.144]$; it is 0.235 at $K=5$. Proposition 1 requires $\sigma<1$. Cross-checks: Krueger's STAR kindergarten comparison gives 0.687, and [JJP (2016)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20847)'s spending elasticity of about 0.7 is close to the implied $\sigma/(1-\eta)\simeq0.61$. Recompute $\sigma$ whenever $K$ or $\eta$ changes.

**Earnings–achievement bridge.** $\Delta\log y_{\rm adult}=\chi\,\Delta a$, with $a$ in student test-score SDs. CFR (2014b) report about 12% higher earnings at 28 per score SD, a conditional association; $\chi=\log1.12$. It is used only for school-driven score changes (SEDA, WTP), read as $b\,s_z/c$; the ability scale $s_z$ comes from the NLSY (section 1, *Scale and $\chi$*).

**SEDA.** The weighted learning-rate gap is 0.0031 SD/grade (SE 0.0030) and the score-level gap 0.2887 SD; with CZ effects, 0.0046 (0.0022) raw and −0.0042 (0.0022) with SES controls. Compare via $\Delta g\simeq\Delta\log Q/(\chi K(1-\eta))$. Growth also reflects families and peers, so the gap is neither a causal teacher effect nor an upper bound on one.

**Willingness to pay.** Compute compensating consumption $a$ from

$$
\mu\log C+\lambda\,\mathbb E[f(h'_0)]=\mu\log(C-a)+\lambda\,\mathbb E[f(h'_1)],
$$

converting BFM's school-average SD (74 points) to student-level SDs before applying $\chi$. Use the \$19.70 structural estimate, not the \$17.30 hedonic one (Table 3 panel B col. 4, SE 5.9). The test is the 1992–93 CLAS, not the CAP, and the 74 points is the SD over BFM's full sample of 242,100 households, not the boundary sample behind the \$19.70. With no student-level SD reported, one school-average SD is $\sqrt{\mathrm{ICC}}\approx0.45$ student SDs, so $x=0.45\chi$ (`wtp_score_sd`). The model computes $a$ for every parent and divides mean $a$ by mean consumption (`school_quality_wtp`).

## 4. Estimation and sensitivity

Minimize $[\mathcal M(\vartheta)-\widehat{\mathcal M}]'W[\mathcal M(\vartheta)-\widehat{\mathcal M}]$ with diagonal inverse-variance weights, adding transport uncertainty to literature moments. Bootstrap whole CZs, re-estimating Table 2 in the same loop. Impose $0<\beta<1$, $\lambda\ge0$, $r_m\ge0$. With the eight Table 3 moments and eight parameters the minimum is a root, so $W$ shapes the search but not the estimate; it matters again whenever a case is overidentified, for example with a validation moment activated (`income_target`) or a parameter fixed (the $r_m$ profile).

- **Identification:** Jacobian rank and singular values; whether $\lambda$, $\Delta B$ and $\beta$ substitute for each other; a profile of $r_m$ including $r_m=0$.
- **Solution:** equilibrium residuals, positive consumption, quadrature accuracy, Nz convergence (5, 9, 15; Table 2, *Ability grid*), multiple starts. `spatial_diagnose.jl --grid Nz=9` (or `nXO=96`, …) re-solves at a point on another grid, keeping the frozen reference.
- **Solver correction (2026-09-29).** The ϵ_T grid was spaced in quantiles and the value functions, their inverses and the teacher integrands were splined in levels of the shocks. After T2b widened σϵ to 0.786, everything above the 0.979 quantile of ϵ_T fell in one grid interval, and that is where teachers are (25–48% of them by gender); the spline missed $W_T$ by up to 5.5 there. The grid is now log-spaced between the same quantiles and every spline and quadrature is in the log shocks (`spatial_continuous.jl` §2, §4). Spline errors in the upper tail fall to $1.3\times10^{-4}$ ($W_T$) and $1.6\times10^{-3}$ ($\log h_T$); the pseudo-sample's mass is 0.99999 (1.00126 before) and the stationarity deviation $7\times10^{-6}$ ($1.3\times10^{-3}$ before); doubling nϵT and nXO to 96 moves every targeted moment by about 0.01 of its scale or less. At $\vartheta_0$ the male teaching share rises from 1.94% to 2.04%, teachers' 90/10 from 3.24 to 3.37 and the CFR effect falls from 0.0128 to 0.0119. Every model number computed before the correction (T1, the T2b handoff check, `runs/fit1-quantile-grid-invalid`) is superseded; $\phi$ (1.0584) and the reference ($\bar C=0.044114$, $\bar h=0.095780$) were recalibrated on the corrected solver. The first stage (`spatial_first_stage.jl`) does not use this grid and is unaffected.
- **External-block consistency (after the first internal fit):** recompute Table 2 moments with teaching selection operating in the full model: both genders' occupation shares conditional on not teaching; non-teacher cell 90/10s and their wage-sample-weighted average; the mother–child score correlation with $c\log Q_l$ (parents' birth locations from the migration kernel, children's from mothers' location choices); and the wage–score slope with teachers included as in the slope sample. Include $Q_l$ and $t_l$ in wages and account for selection among the remaining non-teachers. Compare discrepancies with sampling uncertainty and their effect on the fit and substantive conclusions. If material, re-estimate Table 2 net of the model-implied distortion and refit. Any later selection correction must use the same rule in shares and wage distributions; if it depends on $z$, replace the independent convolution with the selected joint distribution. This verification is not a prerequisite for first-pass T2b.
- **Implementation.** Quantile moments use a weighted pseudo-sample of adults (`micro_sample` in `spatial_moments.jl`): each (gender, birth location, ability node) cell is integrated on 600 midpoints in $\log\epsilon_T$ and $\log X_O^*$, a point carrying its slice's shock mass times the choice probabilities (teach or not, the maximizing occupation, the location logit) and spreading it uniformly between the slice-edge wages, so quantiles are exact up to within-slice curvature. It reproduces the solver's teaching shares to $10^{-6}$; its quantile gaps move by less than $10^{-5}$ between 300 and 1,200 midpoints; and independent Monte Carlo simulations of the occupation and location choices reproduce its teacher 90/10s, wage slope, parent–child correlation, market medians, poverty shares and cell 90/10s within simulation error (checked before the solver correction above, which changes the choice maps both use). Scores are taken at grade $g$ with a loading $(g/K)\,c$ on $\log Q_l$: parents at full exposure (AFQT at 15–23), CNLSY children at $g=4$ and SEDA levels at $g=5.5$ (`Measurement`); with full exposure the child side overstated the $Q$ channel 2–3 times. `occupation_consistency` and `ability_moments` give the §4 counterparts, and `spatial_diagnose.jl --consistency` prints them against the first stage and the data.
- **Teacher 90/10 at $\vartheta_0$.** 3.37 (men 3.14, women 3.44) against 2.77 on the corrected solver, so $\gamma$ must fall in the fit. The T2b scratch diagnostic's 2.79 (men 2.35, women 2.93) is not reproduced by the model counterpart and should be disregarded.
- **Consistency at $\vartheta_0$** (not a fitted point; `runs/dev/theta0`, corrected solver). Non-teacher occupation shares move by at most 0.14 pp for men and 0.66 pp for women (home production 36.2% vs. 35.6%), so ignoring teaching selection in the shares is harmless. The pooled non-teacher 90/10 rises from 3.737 to 3.893, since the $Q$ gap across locations ($\Delta\log Q=0.44$ at $\vartheta_0$) adds dispersion; the latent wage slope is 0.250 against 0.213 (the $Q$ channel again) and the mother–child correlation 0.504 against 0.577. At the fits the $Q$ gap is small ($\Delta\log Q=0.003$ at the first fit, 0.015 at the exactly identified fit): the slope and the correlation match, while the pooled 90/10 falls below the first stage's, to 3.667 and 3.622 (*First internal fit*, *Exactly identified fit*).
- **Residential transitions:** done; the NLSY79 move rate is the $r_m$ target (Table 3), and its flatness in parental income a validation check.

Across its whole box $r_m$ moves the earnings gap by about 0.02 (Jacobian at that fit), so the gap does not identify $r_m$; §2 prescribed validation in that case.
- **Identification (Jacobian at the estimate, standardized by the scales and box widths).** Singular values 245 to 4.19 (condition number 58). $\delta_\kappa$ (salary and FTE gaps), $\Delta B$ (enrollment), $\lambda$ (WTP), $\omega_f$ and $\gamma$ have large unique effects (237, 52, 25, 17, 13); $r_m$ is identified by the move rate (unique norm 5.3); $\tilde\kappa$ and $\beta$ overlap through the teaching shares (projection $R^2$ 0.91 each) but keep unique norms of 8.0 and 8.6, from the CFR effect and the teacher 90/10.
- **External-block consistency at the estimate (§4).** Non-teacher occupation shares move by at most 0.15 pp for men and 0.76 pp for women (home production 36.3% vs. 35.6%); teachers' 90/10s match; the latent wage slope is 0.216 against 0.213 (0.4 SE) and the mother–child correlation 0.5766 against 0.5766. The pooled non-teacher 90/10 is 3.667 against the first stage's 3.737 (men 3.58 vs. 3.65, women 3.77 vs. 3.85): teaching selection and the endogenous investments compress wages slightly once the location gaps vanish. Absorbing it would raise $\sigma_\epsilon$ by about 0.014 (1.8%), inside the Table 5 range (0.766–0.844), whose refits barely move the fit (criteria 5.15 and 5.42), so the first stage stands.
- **Mean wages (T4 check, untargeted).** Against PWGTP-weighted mean log hourly wages from the ACS 2009–13 PUMS (`acs_occupations.py`, which reproduces every workbook count), the model misses the level structure of wages, as expected from a Roy block identified from shares with a common $\sigma_\epsilon$: deviations of cell mean log wages from the market mean correlate 0.09 across the 38 cells (SD 0.17 in the model, 0.26 in the data; the model ranks mechanics and executives highest, the data doctors, lawyers and engineers); the market gender gap is 0.30 against 0.10, so the female wedges that fit women's shares and teaching share imply too low market wages for women; and teachers earn 0.75 (men) and 0.65 (women) log points below the market mean, against 0.04 and 0.07 above it in the data. The model matches the teaching shares with low teacher pay, helped by the schooling miss above ($s_T/s_O=0.75$: with $\gamma<1$ teachers invest less time, which the model values as a non-pecuniary advantage of teaching). Teacher pay relative to market pay is a natural additional target for $\tilde\kappa$ and a check on $\gamma$.
- **Solution checks.** Stationarity deviation $1.4\times10^{-5}$; the population share choosing a location at the consumption floor is $9\times10^{-23}$. Re-solving at the estimate on finer grids leaves every targeted moment unchanged to four digits: the criterion is 5.149 at Nz = 9, 5.150 at Nz = 15 and 5.139 with nϵT = nXO = 96 (`runs/grid-*`), so Nz = 5 and 48-node shock grids stand.

**Interpretation limits.** The model bundles residence, work and school, and local taxes fund teacher payroll only.
